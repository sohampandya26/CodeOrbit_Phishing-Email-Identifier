## 📌 Project Overview

**Phishing** is a form of social engineering in which an attacker impersonates
a trusted person, organization, or service to trick a victim into performing
an unsafe action.

These attacks may attempt to make users:

- Click malicious or suspicious links
- Open harmful attachments
- Reveal passwords, OTPs, or personal information
- Share banking or payment details
- Transfer money or purchase gift cards
- Install malicious software
- Bypass normal security procedures

This project presents a structured **phishing red-flag checklist** and applies
it to **five fictional sample emails** to demonstrate how suspicious messages
can be identified.

> **Educational Disclaimer:** All email addresses, domains, links, names, and
> messages used in this project are fictional and are provided only for
> cybersecurity education and awareness.

## 🎯 Objectives

The main objectives of this task are to:

1. Understand common phishing and social-engineering techniques.
2. Identify suspicious sender addresses and domains.
3. Recognize urgent, threatening, or manipulative language.
4. Detect suspicious or mismatched links.
5. Identify potentially dangerous attachments.
6. Recognize requests for sensitive information or financial actions.
7. Analyze unusual requests from supposedly trusted contacts.
8. Develop practical habits for safely handling suspicious emails.

---

## 🚩 Phishing Red Flags Covered

The project covers the following indicators:

| Red Flag | What to Look For |
|---|---|
| **Urgency & Threats** | Artificial deadlines, account suspension threats |
| **Generic Greetings** | "Dear Customer", "Dear User" |
| **Suspicious Links** | Misspelled domains, shortened URLs, unexpected destinations |
| **Spoofed Senders** | Display name differs from the actual email address |
| **Poor Formatting** | Grammar, spelling, capitalization, or formatting inconsistencies |
| **Unexpected Attachments** | `.zip`, `.exe`, `.rar`, macro-enabled documents |
| **Sensitive Information Requests** | Passwords, OTPs, banking details, identity documents |
| **Too-Good-to-Be-True Offers** | Fake prizes, refunds, inheritances, financial rewards |
| **Unusual Requests** | Secrecy, gift cards, money transfers, bypassing normal procedures |

> A single red flag does not automatically prove that an email is malicious.
> Multiple indicators should be considered together with the context of the
> message.

## 🔍 Key Findings

The sample analysis demonstrates several recurring phishing techniques.

### 1. Urgency and Emotional Pressure

Attackers often create fear or urgency to make the recipient act before
verifying the request.

Examples include:

- Account suspension
- Password expiration
- Limited-time prize claims
- Immediate delivery rescheduling
- Urgent executive requests

### 2. Sender Impersonation

A sender's display name can be misleading. The complete email address and
domain should be checked carefully.

### 3. Suspicious Links

Phishing messages may use shortened URLs, deceptive domains, or links that
lead to fraudulent login pages.

### 4. Malicious or Unexpected Attachments

Unexpected compressed, executable, or macro-enabled files can potentially
deliver malware.

### 5. Sensitive Information Requests

Unexpected requests for passwords, OTPs, banking details, identity documents,
or payment information are strong warning signs.

### 6. Social Engineering

Phishing is not only a technical problem. Attackers manipulate human
emotions such as fear, curiosity, trust, urgency, and greed.

## 🛡️ Safe Email Handling Practices

When an email looks suspicious:

1. **Do not click suspicious links.**
2. **Do not open unexpected attachments.**
3. **Do not provide passwords, OTPs, or financial information.**
4. **Check the complete sender email address.**
5. **Inspect link destinations before interacting with them.**
6. **Verify unusual requests through a separate trusted channel.**
7. **Open the organization's official website or application manually.**
8. **Report suspicious messages through the appropriate reporting process.**
9. **If credentials were submitted, change the affected password immediately
   and follow the organization's incident-response procedure.**
